# XMA Content Rules

Born from real incidents. These are not theoretical — every rule here exists because a specific piece of content already caused us measurable damage.

---

## Rule 1 — Do not name third-party orchestras, employers, or institutions in promotional copy

### Why
On 2026-05-27 we published a Google Business Profile post about Gregory Ayriyan that included the phrase **"concertmaster of the Worcester Symphony Orchestra"** followed by "now performing with Xhoja Music Agency."

Google's automated content moderator removed the post within hours and disabled posting on the entire profile for 7–30 days. The official content policy violation: claiming association with another business/brand without authorization. The orchestra never authorized XMA to use its name as a promotional anchor for our service, even though Greg's role as concertmaster is publicly documented fact.

### Where this rule applies (PROMOTIONAL CONTEXT — banned)
- ❌ GBP posts
- ❌ GBP business description (where the contractor's day-job is juxtaposed with a "book us" CTA)
- ❌ Instagram captions, ads, paid social
- ❌ Email outreach copy (cold pitches, follow-ups)
- ❌ Press releases, partner one-pagers
- ❌ Marketing video voiceovers
- ❌ Print marketing collateral

### Where this rule does NOT apply (BIOGRAPHICAL CONTEXT — fine)
- ✅ Team page bios on `xhojamusicagency.com/team` (factual biography of who the artist is, not a sales pitch)
- ✅ Artist's own personal links from their bio modal (e.g. their personal WordPress, YouTube channel — the user clicks through, association is on their property)
- ✅ Internal records — gig sheets, contractor invoices, accountant handoffs

### The diagnostic test
Before you write *"[Contractor] is/plays/works with [Third-party org]"*, ask: **is this sentence next to a price, a CTA, or a brand-XMA association?** If yes, kill the third-party name and rephrase generically:

| ❌ Don't write | ✅ Do write |
|---|---|
| "Gregory Ayriyan, concertmaster of the Worcester Symphony Orchestra, now available through XMA" | "Gregory Ayriyan — classical violinist with decades of orchestral and solo performance, now available through XMA" |
| "Booked from [Famous Hotel]'s in-house roster" | "Booked from XMA's roster of refined private-event artists" |
| "Featured in [Major Publication]" | (just don't claim it unless you have written endorsement) |

### Safe generic phrasings for classical contractors (reusable)
- "Classical violinist with decades of orchestral and solo work"
- "Conservatory-trained, currently performing across New England"
- "Award-winning composer and performing musician"
- "Berklee-trained pianist" *(safe — Berklee is Alex's alma mater, not an employer claim)*

## Rule 2 — Photo and video subjects need their own consent, even when sent to us by the contractor

### Why
A photo of Greg with the full Worcester Symphony Orchestra was uploaded to XMA's GBP gallery on 2026-05-27 and removed once we caught the issue — Greg sent us the file, but the ~30 other musicians and the orchestra itself never granted promotional use rights.

### The rule
"Contractor sent us the file" gives us THEIR consent only. Every identifiable subject in a photo or video owns their own likeness. For commercial marketing use (which our website, GBP, IG, ads all are):

- 🟢 **Photos of just the contractor alone** → fine
- 🟡 **Photos of the contractor + identifiable clients (e.g. a wedding couple)** → fine *if* the contractor has confirmed those clients granted promotional use. Verify before using.
- 🔴 **Photos of the contractor + other unrelated musicians** → not fine without each musician's consent
- 🔴 **Photos of the contractor inside a recognizable third-party venue or institutional context (orchestra, named hotel, etc.)** → not fine without the institution's consent

### Diagnostic test
Before uploading any contractor-supplied photo to a public XMA surface, ask: **how many identifiable third parties are in this frame?** If more than zero, ask the contractor explicitly: *"Do the people in this photo know XMA may use it for promotional purposes?"* before publishing.

## Rule 3 — Embed YouTube ≠ usage rights

### Why
Embedding a YouTube video on our site uses YouTube's official embed player. Mechanically, it's legally fine — that's what YouTube is designed for. **But** the *underlying content* of the video still carries the same rights questions as a photo.

### The rule
- ✅ Embed YouTube videos featuring the contractor solo or in fully-permitted ensembles
- ✅ Embed YouTube videos contractors send us as their preferred performance reel
- ❌ Embed YouTube videos where third-party institutions (orchestras, named brands, named venues) are the visual or audio focus — even if the contractor is in the video
- ❌ Embed YouTube videos that include identifiable unrelated musicians without their explicit consent

The embed mechanism is fine; the content choice is the call.

## Quick checklist before publishing any external marketing copy/asset

- [ ] Does the copy name a third-party orchestra, employer, brand, venue, or institution? → Remove or genericize.
- [ ] Does the visual include identifiable third parties (musicians, clients, venue staff)? → Verify per-subject consent with the contractor.
- [ ] Is the copy framing the contractor as XMA's hire (price, CTA, "book us") next to a third-party name? → Always re-check Rule 1.
- [ ] If reusing yesterday's draft, scan for ANY mention of Worcester Symphony Orchestra or "concertmaster of…" — these phrases are banned in promotional context.
